# Psychological realism

These are writing heuristics, not diagnoses or proven conversion laws. Validate audience hypotheses against real customer language where available.

## Situation before emotion

An observable action lets people recognize themselves. An asserted inner state can make them feel misread.

Generic: "You're overwhelmed and desperate to regain control."

Concrete, if supported by the brief: "You check the spreadsheet, open the message thread, and still can't tell whether the order went out."

This is an illustrative scene, not a testimonial. Do not borrow it when it does not fit the audience.

## Preserve competence

Explain why the current approach was reasonable before discussing its limitations. A familiar workaround may have lower switching costs than your product. Acknowledge those costs.

Weak: "You've been doing everything wrong."

Better: "The spreadsheet was enough when you were tracking a few orders. The difficulty is keeping it current when three people are editing it."

## Distinguish concerns

| Buyer language | Possible concern to investigate | Useful response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I tried something like this | Repeating a costly mistake | Explain a relevant difference and its evidence |
| I don't have time | Setup, workload, competing priorities | State actual effort and dependencies |
| It's expensive | Budget, value uncertainty, comparison | Give real price, terms, fair comparison |
| Will this work for me? | Fit and relevance of evidence | Explain prerequisites, cases, exclusions |
| I need to think | Unanswered questions or preference | Clarify uncertainty and preserve choice |

Not every objection conceals an emotional issue. An unaffordable offer may simply be unaffordable. Never portray borrowing money, ignoring a partner, or rushing as proof of commitment.

## Earn belief changes

Move from an acceptable observation to a causal explanation, supporting evidence, and a limited conclusion. Recognizing the problem does not establish that this product solves it.

A recording can demonstrate a feature in that example. It cannot establish that every customer saves ten hours a week. One customer story does not establish typical results. A refund policy may reduce financial risk but does not erase time, effort, and every possible downside.

## Make the future believable

Describe an appropriate moment after adoption: locating an order, completing a task, enjoying the fit of a garment, or understanding a next step. Do not automatically promise wealth, status, freedom, approval, or a new identity.

Use uncertainty precisely. "Designed to" cannot disguise an unsupported claim. Conditions must be meaningful.

## Natural speech

Write as a competent person explaining a useful offer to someone they respect. Vary sentence length. Explain unfamiliar terms. Let transitions answer the next natural question.

Overwritten: "Discover the revolutionary solution engineered to seamlessly transform your daily workflow."

Natural: "Here's what happens when a new order comes in."

The natural line earns its place only when the script actually explains or shows that process. Plain language must still carry information.

Avoid constant fragments and verbal tics such as "look," "listen," and "here's the thing." Do not imitate spontaneity with filler. A direct statement of a real limitation is often more credible than another reassurance.
