# Example: a fictional desk organizer

Everything below is fictional input for trying the skill. These are not claims about a real product or evidence of performance.

```text
Use $vsl-scriptwriter to write a 2-minute checkout VSL using this fictional brief.
Treat the supplied specifications as facts within the example, but do not add
customer results, certifications, statistics, or testimonials.

Product: ClearDesk Tray, a modular desk organizer.
Audience: People working at small home desks who frequently move pens,
charging cables, and notebooks to make room for their laptop.
Offer: One tray with three movable dividers. $39, single purchase.
Mechanism: Dividers slide into slots so users can change compartment sizes.
Specifications: 30 cm wide, 18 cm deep, 4 cm tall. Wipe-clean surface.
Evidence: A planned on-camera demonstration of the dividers and the tray
holding everyday desk items. No customer testimonials or durability tests.
Objections: Will it fit? Is setup complicated? Does it actually hold my items?
Limits: It does not include a charger and cannot hold a laptop.
Shipping: Ships within 3 business days. Delivery timing varies by destination.
Returns: Returns accepted within 30 days of delivery; buyer pays return shipping.
Traffic: Visitors who clicked an ad showing the tray being rearranged.
CTA: Choose a color and add the tray to the cart.
Voice: Calm, practical, conversational English.
Urgency: None. No expiring promotion or limited-stock claim.

Include useful demonstration cues outside the spoken narration.
```

Review the result for a clear explanation, correctly stated limitations, no invented proof, one main CTA, realistic runtime, and no em dashes. The product should be sold through usefulness rather than exaggerated shame about a messy desk.
