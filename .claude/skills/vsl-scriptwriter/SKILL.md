# VSL Scriptwriter

Write a complete sales conversation that earns the next buying decision. Adapt to the actual product, audience, evidence, and sales process. A skincare product, enterprise tool, and coaching program must not sound like the same offer with different nouns.

Use original wording. Apply the ideas and functional structure in the references without copying extended passages or imitating a creator's personal voice. Read [sources-and-frameworks.md](references/sources-and-frameworks.md) when choosing a structure or explaining attribution. Do not describe this skill as the verbatim framework from $100M Leads: the claimed book passage has not been verified. If relevant book pages become available, inspect them and reconcile the structure instead of guessing.

## Establish the buying decision

Use existing briefs, product pages, interviews, reviews, and sales transcripts before asking questions. Separate confirmed facts, customer language, and editorial hypotheses.

Find the product, specific buyer, desired outcome, mechanism, offer contents, available proof, price and payment terms, awareness level, traffic source, duration, voice, and ONE primary next action. Identify actual guarantees, return conditions, delivery timing, and restrictions where relevant.

If the product, buyer, or next action is missing, ask one compact set of questions. Otherwise draft using explicitly stated editorial assumptions. Never assume business facts such as prices, savings, testimonials, results, credentials, scarcity, or refund rights. Omit unsupported claims or put editorial markers outside spoken copy. If missing facts prevent a recordable script, label it a working draft and list the precise missing inputs. Do not turn a missing voice preference into a long intake interview.

For rewrites, preserve confirmed facts and the user's required structure. Diagnose the friction before changing it. Do not silently redesign the offer.

## Model a believable buyer

Read [buyer-psychology.md](references/buyer-psychology.md) for new audiences or substantial rewrites. Establish:

- The ordinary situation that makes the problem noticeable.
- What they tried and why trying it made sense.
- The outcome they want and its practical or social meaning.
- What they risk by buying: money, time, autonomy, reputation, or another failed attempt.
- Their reasonable alternatives, including doing nothing.
- What evidence and information would help them consider the next step.

Treat inferred inner thoughts as hypotheses. Do not assign shame, trauma, desperation, or private fears without evidence. A buying objection can be correct. Answer it honestly rather than trying to defeat the buyer.

Choose one central argument: this product helps this buyer achieve this supported outcome through this understandable process, subject to these real conditions. Each section should strengthen or qualify it.

## Choose and adapt the structure

Use the primary-source compact structure in the reference by default. Use the extended sequence when the buyer needs more narrative and offer explanation. For a familiar local service, consider the simpler factual structure. Keep source attribution distinct.

These writing jobs are this skill's original implementation guidance. Merge or reorder them to match awareness and runtime:

1. **Earn attention.** Open with a recognizable situation or relevant demonstration. Match the promise made by the ad or page that brought the viewer here. Give a credible reason to continue. Avoid greetings and unrelated biography.
2. **Explain the problem.** Describe an observable moment, its consequence, and the gap left by the current approach. Explain why a reasonable previous attempt may not address this situation. Do not claim all alternatives fail.
3. **Explain the mechanism.** Show what the product does, how someone uses it, and why it can affect the outcome. A branded method name is not an explanation. For simple products, a demonstration may do this entire job.
4. **Earn belief.** Put evidence beside its claim. Use actual demonstrations, documented specifications, relevant case evidence, or attributable testimony. Include material conditions. An illustration explains; it does not prove effectiveness. With no outcome evidence, narrow the promise and demonstrate observable functionality.
5. **Make the offer concrete.** Explain what buyers receive and what participation requires. Connect components to actual needs. Use fair price comparisons. Do not invent inflated bonus values, discounts, or reference prices.
6. **Resolve friction.** Address the objections that could stop this audience. Answer directly, explain the reason, and supply evidence or a limitation. Distinguish uncertainty from a genuine lack of budget or fit.
7. **Show a plausible after-state.** Describe a useful change the buyer could notice, preserving real conditions and effort. Do not turn a modest benefit into guaranteed transformation of their whole life.
8. **Clarify the next step.** Give one primary action, what happens immediately afterward, and its commitment. Include only confirmed guarantees and genuine time or capacity limits. Close without manufactured urgency when there is no deadline.

Do not force pain into enjoyment, gifting, hobby, or luxury purchases. Desire, taste, craft, convenience, and fit can carry the argument.

## Adapt to product and funnel

| Situation | Emphasize |
| --- | --- |
| Physical product | Demonstration, fit, use conditions, materials, delivery and return facts |
| Software | Recognizable workflow, observable output, setup, integrations, recurring terms |
| Service or consulting | Scope, process, responsibilities, suitability, relevant evidence |
| Course or coaching | Specific capability, practice required, access, support, prerequisites |
| Membership | Recurring value, participation expectations, renewal and cancellation |
| Familiar local service | Service area, availability, scope, trust, practical examples |
| Novel or technical product | Clear explanation; separate demonstrated properties from proposed benefits |

Cold traffic usually needs more context. Product-aware viewers may need comparison, proof, and terms immediately. A booking VSL sells the fit and usefulness of a conversation. A checkout VSL must resolve enough purchase questions to support buying without one. Use the user's actual funnel. Do not hide a known price merely because it is high.

For health, finance, and regulated categories, verify material claims and applicable requirements when needed. Do not turn a sales framework into unsupported health outcomes, investment promises, or implied approvals.

## Write for a real voice

- No em dash character anywhere in the deliverable, including headings, notes, and alternatives. Do not substitute double hyphens as theatrical pauses. Rewrite with a full stop, comma, colon, or new sentence.
- Address one person in the requested language. Use contractions where natural, familiar words, varied sentence lengths, and paragraphs that can be spoken comfortably.
- Preserve the speaker's actual register. Humanizing does not mean random slang, filler, fake stumbles, profanity, or deliberate mistakes.
- Prefer concrete actions to abstractions. Replace "gain clarity" with what someone will understand or do differently. Do not invent measurable improvements to sound specific.
- Remove stock expressions such as "unlock your potential," "game-changing," "secret they don't want you to know," and "take it to the next level" unless explicitly needed.
- Remove repetitive questions, artificial suspense, automatic three-part slogans, and repeated "It's not X, it's Y" constructions. Let transitions follow the buyer's next reasonable question.
- Use a founder story only when supplied or verified and relevant. Never manufacture personal experiences, testimonials, or customer dialogue.
- Match emotional intensity to the purchase. Disclose a real tradeoff when useful; do not perform fake vulnerability.

## Deliver

Unless asked for another format, provide:

1. A compact brief: audience, central argument, primary action, material assumptions.
2. Three distinct opening options. Choose one for the full script.
3. The complete spoken script with short section labels outside narration. Include only production cues that help show the product or evidence. An outline is not a script.
4. Actual spoken word count and estimated runtime. Calculate from the narration, using an explicit planning rate, normally 130 to 160 words per minute for conversational English. Allow additional time for demonstrations. Adjust for language and delivery.
5. Brief recording or evidence notes only for items needing verification, insertion, or production. Keep citations and unresolved facts out of the speaker's mouth.

If duration is unspecified, select the shortest length that adequately supports the decision and state the choice. Do not default every product to a webinar. For a script-only request, omit extra packaging while retaining essential factual caveats outside narration.

## Final editing pass

Read aloud mentally and repair breathless clauses and unnatural transitions. Check:

- The viewer can quickly identify relevance and a reason to continue.
- The mechanism is understandable and evidence supports the actual promise.
- Concerns sound like this buyer; answers preserve legitimate tradeoffs.
- Prices, terms, qualifications, results, delivery, guarantees, and urgency match source facts.
- The ending gives one action and accurately describes what follows.
- There are no fabricated stories, unsupported statistics, unexplained leaps, or disguised placeholders in a supposedly recordable script.
- Actual word count fits the requested runtime.
- A literal search for Unicode U+2014 returns zero matches across delivered text. Rewrite rather than blindly replacing punctuation.

When auditing, prioritize changes that improve comprehension, credibility, and decision readiness. Do not promise a conversion lift without experiment results.
