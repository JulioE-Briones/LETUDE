01 · What we understood

A city website that looks like Monroe, not like a template

Monroe's current website runs on CivicLive, which loses support on September 30, 2027. The City wants a distinctive, photo-forward site aligned with its brand guide, where residents can pay, apply, report a problem, find a job or check a calendar without hunting through menus.

Behind the scenes, staff need a vendor-hosted CMS with an easy editor, permissions and approvals, and a clean move of most existing pages and PDFs.

What the RFP asks for

  • Distinctive design built on the City's brand guide.
  • Clear resident tasks: payments, requests, reports, jobs, calendars.
  • Vendor-hosted CMS with WYSIWYG editing, roles and approvals.
  • Migration of about 90% of content and PDFs.
  • WCAG 2.1 AA, search, automatic translation, emergency banner, document library and SSL.
  • Launch by September 1, 2027, on a 5-year base contract.

05 · Accessibility, translation, search and security

Accessible, findable and secure by default

Accessibility and translation

  • WCAG 2.1 AA on every template: contrast, keyboard navigation, alt text, accessible forms and tables.
  • Testing with automated tools and manual keyboard and screen reader checks before launch, with a written report.
  • PDF triage: the most-used PDFs become accessible web pages; the rest are flagged for remediation.
  • Automatic translation of every page, with a visible language selector.
  • Site search across pages and documents.

Hosting and security

  • Managed WordPress hosting on SOC 2 Type II–audited infrastructure in U.S. data centers.
  • SSL, web application firewall, daily off-site backups and uptime monitoring.
  • Two-factor login and least-privilege roles for every editor.
  • Core, theme and plugin updates applied and tested monthly.
  • TWO&HUMAN does not hold its own SOC 2 report; we rely on the hosting provider's audited controls and document ours.

06 · Migration plan

About 90% of the content moves, nothing gets lost

1 · Inventory

Every page and PDF on the current site, with traffic, owner and date.

2 · Decide

Staff mark each item: keep, update, merge or retire, aiming at the RFP's 90%.

3 · Move

Pages and documents migrated into the new structure, with metadata for search and the library.

4 · Redirect

A 301 map for every changed address, tested before launch, so links and search results keep working.

The City keeps the full inventory spreadsheet. During the 60-day stabilization period after launch, we fix any broken link or migration issue at no cost.
