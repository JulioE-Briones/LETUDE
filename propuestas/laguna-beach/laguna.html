01 · Understanding Laguna Beach

A city with a strong identity deserves a website that feels like Laguna.

Laguna Beach is known far beyond Orange County for its coastline, its canyons and an arts heritage that includes the Festival of Arts and the Pageant of the Masters. Its website is often the first place people meet the City, and it should carry that character while making everyday tasks simple.

The City Manager's Office has made public communication a priority, including its 2026 solicitations for communications support. This project gives that work a home that every department can keep current.

Residents

Pay, apply, report a problem, follow Council meetings and get emergency updates.

Visitors

Parking, beaches, events and the information the City is responsible for, in a few taps.

Businesses

Permits, licenses and planning information without calling City Hall.

Department editors

Publish their own updates quickly, with the system protecting design and accessibility.

Audience priorities will be confirmed with City staff and site analytics during discovery.

Preliminary design direction

Color and type drawn from the coastline, the canyons and the arts. Final palette follows the City's brand standards and contrast tests.

04 · The recommended solution

The Laguna Publishing System: four parts that work as one.

01

Task-first architecture

Navigation and homepage built around residents' top tasks, validated with staff and analytics.

02

A design system with character

Typography, color and photography direction rooted in Laguna, applied to reusable, accessible templates.

03

Governed WordPress

Department roles, locked patterns, revision history and an approval step before anything goes live.

04

Accessibility built in

An open-source in-editor checker such as Editoria11y, monthly automated scans and a written report.

How a department page goes live
Draft

The editor builds the page from an approved pattern.

Check

The checker flags missing alt text, skipped headings or low contrast.

Approve

The department lead reviews and approves.

Publish

The page goes live with a full revision history.

Monitor

Monthly scans catch issues across the whole site.

11 · The questions a careful committee should ask

We would ask them too. Here are our answers.

"Can a team outside the U.S. keep a City deadline?"

Argentina is 5 hours ahead of Pacific Time in winter, so our afternoon covers the City's morning. Weekly written status, video calls in Pacific hours and replies within one business day. Launch is planned two weeks early.

"What evidence do we have before we sign?"

The concepts in this proposal, a working session with your team and a clickable prototype in week 4. Payments follow approved milestones, so the City sees the work before it pays for it.

"Who carries the accessibility risk?"

We build and test to WCAG 2.1 AA, deliver a written report and leave the checker inside the editor, so the standard holds after handover.

"What if one founder is unavailable?"

Both founders work on every project. Code lives in a shared repository, documentation is written as we go, and every credential is in the City's name from day one.

"Will we be locked in?"

No. Standard open-source WordPress, no proprietary page builder. The City owns domain, content, code and design files, and any qualified developer can take over.

"Contracts, insurance and payments?"

Invoices in U.S. dollars, IRS Form W-8BEN-E as a foreign vendor, and certificates of insurance as the City requires verify timing.

Our commitments

Fixed price. No change orders for anything in this scope.

Pay on approval. Three milestones the City signs off.

60 days of stabilization after launch at no extra cost.

The City's name on the domain, code and every account.
