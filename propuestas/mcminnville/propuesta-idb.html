01Executive summary

The IDB does not need a prettier government page. It needs a recruitment tool: a site that lets a site selector or a corporate real estate executive decide in a few minutes that Warren County belongs on the shortlist, find an available site or building, and contact the IDB. Today the state-provided template cannot do that, and the county's advantages in workforce, infrastructure, logistics and utilities stay hidden.

We propose to build the brand and the website together, in that order, so the new identity shapes every page from the start:

  • Recruitment-first architecture, with paths for site selectors first, then existing industry, real estate and developers, and community and workforce.
  • Data-forward content: a quick-facts dashboard, downloadable community data and a Sites & Buildings section connected to TVASites.com.
  • A refreshed industrial brand: three logo concepts, tagline options and a complete style guide.
  • Built to last: WordPress, WCAG 2.2 AA, structured data for AI-powered search, managed hosting with clear service levels, and every account in the IDB's name.

TWO&HUMAN is a two-person studio founded in 2026 in Argentina, working remotely on U.S. Central Time business hours (we are three hours ahead in winter). We are a new firm, and we say so plainly. Design, content and visual assets

  • Distinctive, custom design on WordPress: a custom theme built from the new brand, not a generic template. Mobile-first, with strong calls to action on every page.
  • Content creation: we write the value propositions, headlines and page copy from the data and interviews the IDB provides, and the IDB approves every page.
  • SEO best practices: page structure, metadata, internal links and fast load times.
  • Visual assets (optional, separately priced): two on-site shoot days for photography and drone imagery of sites, industry, workforce and quality of life, by a local provider we coordinate and confirm with the IDB. The IDB receives full usage rights. If the IDB already has suitable photos, we use them instead.

Technical requirements

  • Open-source CMS: WordPress, with an editing experience designed so IDB staff update news, facts, documents and pages without a developer.
  • Security and performance: HTTPS/TLS, firewall and malware scanning, image optimization and caching.
  • Analytics: Google Analytics 4, with events for the actions that matter (site views, downloads, inquiries).
  • AI-powered search: schema.org structured data (organization, places, sites, FAQs and news), an entity-based content architecture, and short fact-dense summaries at the top of key pages, so AI search tools can extract and cite the county's data accurately.
  • Migration and redirects: current content reviewed and migrated, with redirects that preserve existing links and search visibility.
  • Training and documentation: a recorded session for designated staff, a written guide, and full administrative access.
  • Launch and warranty: launch assistance and testing, then a 60-day post-launch warranty for defects, twice the minimum requested.

07Accessibility testing (WCAG 2.2 AA)

  1. Design: color contrast, focus states, target sizes and readable typography checked in the designs, before development.
  2. Automated testing: axe and WAVE on every template and key page.
  3. Manual testing: keyboard-only navigation, screen readers (NVDA and VoiceOver), zoom to 200%, forms, error messages and embedded content.
  4. Remediation: issues logged, fixed and retested before launch. The IDB receives the testing log and an accessibility statement page.
  5. Training: staff learn to keep new content accessible (headings, image descriptions, accessible PDFs).

08Tennessee/TVA listing integration (TVASites.com)

  • Primary approach: embed the TVASites.com listings for Warren County inside a branded Sites & Buildings page, with our header, filters, introduction and contact call to action around the embed. In discovery we confirm with TVA which embed elements are available and how far they can be styled.
  • Known limitations to plan for: third-party embeds may not take our fonts and colors, may have their own accessibility and mobile-display limits, and depend on TVA's service. We test the embed on mobile and with assistive technologies, and we document any limitation we cannot fix.
  • Alternative if embedding is not feasible: native WordPress pages for featured sites and buildings (photos, acreage, utilities, map, distance to highways, downloadable one-page PDF spec sheet), each linking to the full TVASites.com listing. IDB staff update them from the CMS.
